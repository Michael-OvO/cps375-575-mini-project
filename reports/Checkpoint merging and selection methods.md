# Gate the hard tail, average the end

**Bottom line:** For rl1, the methods worth building are cheap, and the honest expected gain from all checkpoint tricks together is about **0 to +1 point per benchmark**. The one hybrid with a real chance of moving MATH-500 and MBPP is **HPT-style gated SFT**: when all 8 rollouts of a prompt fail, train on a verified teacher answer for that prompt instead of throwing the group away. It costs roughly 3-10% of the run, uses only losses Tinker already has, and is the best-performing hybrid in its own benchmarks at 1.5B-8B ([HPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04419v2)). If staff approve the 397B teacher and the pre-flight in the rl1 spec passes, a **group-masked reverse-KL teacher term** (KDRL/OPD) has the strongest evidence per dollar of anything reviewed, and it is the only method with direct IFEval evidence (IFEval recovered from 45% to 83% in [Thinking Machines' OPD study](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)). On the checkpoint side, build two things: an **exact ΔW-space average of the last 3-5 saved checkpoints**, uploaded through Tinker's experimental external-weights API, and **one conditional v4↔rl1 interpolation** as insurance against a domain regressing. Pick among them with a pre-registered, smoothed, paired dev protocol, and open the vault once. The reward stays pure RLVR. Every measured way of gating or multiplying a verifiable IF reward by a judge or RM score lowered IFEval ([Pyatkin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02833v3)). One caveat applies to everything below: almost every cited gain comes from weaker starting models (zero-RL Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Gemma, Llama-3.1-8B), from full fine-tuning, from math only, and from runs of 500-9,000 steps. A strong r32 LoRA SFT model doing 200 RL steps should expect a fraction of those numbers.

## Dev noise, not merge cleverness, sets the ceiling

Start with what our dev sets can resolve. The rl1 spec uses greedy dev splits of 325 math, 337 code and 461 IF items. At about 10% discordance between candidates, the paired two-standard-error threshold is **about 3.5 points per area**, and 80% power needs about 4.5 points (rl1 spec, `/Users/michael/Documents/Github/cps375-575-mini-project/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-16-rl-design.md`, Section 10). The published effects of checkpoint averaging, interpolation and extrapolation after RL are mostly **−0.5 to +1 point**. So dev cannot confirm any single merge as better than the best native checkpoint. It can only catch merges that broke something. That fact drives the whole design: choose methods whose expected sign is known from prior evidence, fix a default before looking, and use dev mainly as a regression filter.

Picking the best of many candidates makes this worse. A local Monte-Carlo simulation (our own computation, not a literature figure) found that with 10-40 truly equal candidates at 70-80% accuracy and 300-500 items, the winner's dev score is **inflated by about 1.6-4.4 points** under realistic item correlation. With a smooth true accuracy curve, though, argmax selection loses only about **0.35 points** of true accuracy, and choosing the argmax of a **3-checkpoint moving average** cut that to 0.24. The danger is not picking a bad checkpoint. It is believing that a merge "beats v4 by 2 points" when it does not. This matches the standard results that an unbiased estimate of a selected model needs data unseen by selection ([Cawley & Talbot](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html)) and that averaging scores across neighbouring checkpoints improves development decisions ([Ai2 Signal and Noise](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13144)). The paired-difference method for comparing models on shared items is laid out in [Anthropic's eval-statistics note](https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals).

The starting point matters too. Section 1 of the rl1 spec shows that the reachable zone is the "flipping" items, the ones some SFT version got right: 61 MATH-500, 30 MBPP and 73 IFEval items that v4 misses but an earlier version solved. That headroom exists, but short RL sharpens behaviour toward modes that already pass; it does not add capability ([Yue et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13837v4)). Checkpoint methods act on top of whatever RL gains, so their headroom is smaller still.

## A ranked shortlist of five methods that clear the bar

The table summarizes the ranking. Each method is then covered in detail. "Distinctive" means it would read as a deliberate, uncommon choice in a course report rather than a default.

| Rank | Method | Type | Extra cost | Our honest expected gain | Gated on | Distinctive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HPT gated SFT on all-fail groups | In-RL hybrid | ~3-10% of run (~$3-8) | MATH/MBPP +0.5 to +2, IF ±0 (a guess) | Nothing | Yes |
| 2 | Group-masked 397B reverse-KL teacher term | In-RL hybrid | ≤ $0.65/step (spec cap) | IF +1 to +3 over RLVR alone (a guess) | Staff approval, pre-flight | Yes |
| 3 | Exact ΔW tail average (last 3-5 saves) | Post-hoc merge | ~$1-3 (export, upload, one dev eval) | 0 to +1 aggregate, mostly lower variance | Upload round trip | Yes (the engineering) |

### 1. HPT-style gated SFT turns dead groups into signal

**Mechanism.** For each prompt, sample G rollouts and score them as usual. If at least one passes, use the RL loss. If none pass, the group's centered advantage is exactly zero and the rl1 spec drops it. Instead, train on a supervised demonstration for that prompt. HPT's gate is γ=0 for Qwen models, meaning SFT only when all 8 rollouts fail. At 1.5B, γ=0 scored **41.9** against 38.7 and 39.0 for looser gates ([HPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04419v2)). ReLIFT and BREAD apply the same idea, putting demonstration signal only where on-policy RL has none. ReLIFT used **13% of the demonstration data** for +5.2 average over zero-RL ([ReLIFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07527)). BREAD used **under 40% of ground-truth traces** and trained about 3× faster than GRPO ([BREAD](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/118358)).

**Evidence.** On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, HPT reaches **52.7 in-distribution / 62.3 OOD**, against SFT→GRPO 46.5/49.3, LUFFY 49.8/60.1 and GRPO 43.1/58.8. On Llama-3.1-8B it reaches 18.2 against GRPO's 9.6 ([HPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04419v2)). The same family of papers shows the trap to avoid: a fixed, always-on SFT loss during RL was LUFFY's worst variant, at **40.1 ID / 46.0 OOD versus 45.5/57.3 for pure RL**, and also the most expensive, because rigid imitation produced long generations ([LUFFY](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14945)). If any supervised term runs on every step, keep it small: CHORD's μ=0.1 with per-token weight p(1−p) beat SFT→RL by about 4 AMC points and 5 MMLU-Pro points on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct ([CHORD](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11408v3)).

**Expected gain for us.** These are zero-RL math numbers from models that never saw the target distribution. v4 has already been trained on our teacher pool, so re-imitating those answers teaches less. The value sits in the hard tail: level-4/5 math and hard code, where v4 gets 0/8. **I estimate +0.5 to +2 points on MATH-500 and MBPP, and roughly zero on IFEval.** No paper tests this starting point, so treat that as a guess, and remember it is below what dev can confirm.

**Cost and how it fits rl1.** There is no extra sampling. Each step adds at most one teacher sequence per all-fail group, against 512 sampled sequences, so the extra training cost is about **3-10% of the run**. In Tinker this can be a second `forward_backward` call with `cross_entropy` before the same `optim_step`. It can also be written as an `importance_sampling` datum: set the sampler logprobs to the current policy's logprobs on the teacher tokens (one cheap prefill) and the advantage to μ, or to μ·p(1−p) per token for CHORD-φ. At ratio 1 that gives exactly a weighted NLL gradient. This mapping is my own derivation and needs a unit test. Use a separate μ per domain. Define "all-fail" as max R = 0 for math and code. For IF, where reward is partial credit, define it as no rollout meeting every constraint under strict scoring. Require the teacher answer to pass the spec's IF quality gate.

**Risks.** Outputs can grow longer through teacher-style imitation, so watch the per-domain length pause rule. Memorization is a risk if the demonstration is an answer v4 already trained on, so prefer pool answers held out of v4's SFT where they exist. A last concern comes from the OPD study: SFT on the model's own samples degraded IFEval at every practical LR ([TM OPD](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)). That warns against self-SFT, not against teacher demonstrations, but it argues for keeping μ small on IF.

### 2. A group-masked 397B teacher term has the best evidence per dollar, if allowed

**Mechanism.** On-policy distillation lets the student sample while the teacher scores every token. The per-token reverse KL becomes an advantage inside Tinker's `importance_sampling` loss, and the teacher call is prefill only (`compute_logprobs`). KDRL adds this term to the verifiable reward, advantage_t = A_group + β(log π_T − log π_θ) with β≈2e-3 (annealed from 5e-3). It found that a separate loss term worked while folding the KL into the scalar reward collapsed early. **Group-level masking**, which applies the teacher term only to all-fail groups, kept accuracy and shortened outputs ([KDRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02208v1)).

**Evidence.** For the Qwen3-8B-Base student, OPD reached **AIME'24 74.4 at 1,800 GPU-hours**, against **67.6 for RL at 17,920 GPU-hours**. A LoRA r32 student's gap to full fine-tuning shrank from 13% after SFT to 6% after OPD. In a forgetting experiment, OPD restored **IFEval from 45% to 83%**. The Tinker cookbook now uses **Qwen3.5-9B-Base** as the student, which is our base ([TM OPD](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)). KDRL at 1.5B scored **57.2** (annealed) against 56.1 for pure reverse-KL distillation and 54.6 for GRPO ([KDRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02208v1)).

**Expected gain for us.** No study reports OPD *gains*, as opposed to recovered losses, on IFEval or MBPP at 9B, and none uses a 397B Qwen3.5 teacher. The rl1 spec scopes the term to IF only, the domain where RL most risks over-optimizing the checker. **I estimate +1 to +3 IFEval points over RLVR alone, and little on math and code**, as a guess.

**Cost and how it fits rl1.** This is already Section 8b of the rl1 spec, with a pre-flight: re-render into the Qwen3.5 template, no thinking pull, log-ratio AUC ≥ 0.65 between reward-1 and reward-0 samples, measured cost ≤ $0.65 per step, and staff approval. The research adds one suggestion: if the IF-only term passes, apply the term to *all-fail* groups in math and code as well (KDRL's group masking). That sends teacher signal to exactly the prompts HPT targets, at a fraction of the token cost of scoring everything. Do not run both HPT and the teacher term on the same groups in the first pass. Use HPT where there is no teacher, and the teacher term where there is one.

**Risks.** Tokenizer and template mismatch (see open questions). Teacher price, which could plausibly add 50-150% to run cost if left unmasked. Possible style pull toward the teacher's thinking-mode habits. And a policy risk if the teacher is not approved, in which case the run stays RLVR-only.

### 3. An exact ΔW tail average is the cheapest robust checkpoint candidate

**Mechanism.** Average the weight updates of the last 3-5 saved checkpoints in a stable window, uniformly. Do it in ΔW = (α/r)·B·A space, not by averaging the A and B factors. Factor averaging gives mean(B)·mean(A), which differs from mean(B·A) by a cross-covariance term ([Crowded in B-Space](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16826)). Concatenating the factors gives the exact average at rank 32·k. To pass Tinker's upload validation at rank 32, take a per-module rank-32 truncated SVD of the averaged ΔW. Within one low-LR trajectory the error should be small, because A barely moves ([Zhu et al., LoRA asymmetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16842)), but measure it: compute ‖mean(BA) − SVD₃₂‖_F / ‖mean(BA)‖_F per layer before uploading.

**Evidence.** The closest analogue is an unreviewed but systematic DPO study on Qwen3 4B/8B/14B and Llama-3.1-8B with about 10 saves each. Averaging all saves was the most robust recipe: **+0.30 z over honest held-out checkpoint selection** and +0.11 z over the last checkpoint. It **hurt in 1 of 4 models** (Qwen3-4B, −0.22 z). In 87% of cases some single checkpoint matched or beat the merge. Gains came mostly from runs that declined late ([OpenEuroLLM #434](https://github.com/OpenEuroLLM/Taskboard/issues/434)). Larger effects come from pretraining at constant LR, where checkpoint averaging raised HumanEval from 31.1 to 36.6 ([PMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12082v1)). Averaging recovers much, but not all, of what an LR cooldown buys ([Hägele et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18392v3)). rl1 runs at a constant 2e-5, which is the regime where averaging acts as an implicit cooldown. That LR is low for LoRA, though, so iterate noise and the possible gain are small. **No paper reports post-hoc averaging of RLVR checkpoints on MATH, IFEval or MBPP.**

**Expected gain for us.** **0 to +1 point aggregate.** It is mainly a variance reducer and insurance against a late decline. It will not create capability.

**Cost and how it fits rl1.** rl1 saves `weights/` every 10 steps. Exporting each with `save_weights_external`, merging locally on CPU, uploading through `external_weights_upload_start`/`finish`, and calling `save_weights_for_sampler` costs a few short sessions plus one dev eval (about $1.2 per candidate from the spec's ledger). The upload API exists in tinker 0.39.0 but is marked `@experimental`. It accepts only files "exactly what `save_weights_external` exports" for the same base model and LoraConfig (`/Users/michael/Documents/Github/cps375-575-mini-project/.venv/lib/python3.11/site-packages/tinker/lib/public_interfaces/rest_client.py`, lines 505-597). The public docs describe only download ([Tinker weights tutorial](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tutorials/core-concepts/weights/)). Before any merge, do a round trip: upload the unmodified v4 export and confirm its greedy dev outputs match v4.

**Risks.** Averaging across a reward-hacking or format-drift phase lets the average inherit part of it. Mixing two answer conventions can tip greedy decoding into neither, so check format-compliance and truncation rates on the merge, not just accuracy. Capabilities that peak at different steps can make the average worse per benchmark while better on the sum ([OpenEuroLLM #434](https://github.com/OpenEuroLLM/Taskboard/issues/434)). The upload API could also change or disappear.

**Why it stands out.** The ΔW-exact merge, the SVD back to rank 32 and the round-trip validation on a hosted LoRA service are uncommon. Most course projects either average factors naively or skip merging.

### 4. Interpolating back toward v4 is insurance, not a gain

**Mechanism.** θ = θ_base + ΔW_v4 + λ(ΔW_rl1 − ΔW_v4) with λ≈0.6, built with the same ΔW, SVD and upload path. This is WiSE-FT, or the post-hoc version of WARP's interpolate-toward-init step and of Elastic Reset.

**Evidence.** Interpolating SFT and RLHF weights gave the best alignment-versus-forgetting Pareto front at OpenLLaMA-3B and Mistral-7B ([Lin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06256v4)). Everywhere it was tested on reasoning, though, SFT↔RL interpolation traces a trade-off *between* the endpoints rather than beating the RL endpoint ([Synergy Dilemma](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07562v1)). The positive WiSE-FT reasoning result is for early-versus-late SFT checkpoints, and it mostly recovers pass@k, not greedy pass@1 ([Dang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10478v3)). RLVR updates are close to rank-1 and nearly linear in time (R² > 0.98 for most tensors on Qwen3-4B/8B-Base) ([RELEX](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21468v1)), so λ=0.5 behaves roughly like an earlier RL checkpoint. That means picking an earlier saved checkpoint does most of the same job for free.

**Expected gain for us.** **About zero on average.** It is worth building only if dev shows one area more than 1 SE below v4 while another gained, or if math truncation at 4,096 tokens rises. The v4 model is our "short" model, and long-to-short merging at 7B cut length by about 50% at parity ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20641v2)). In those cases it plausibly recovers 1-3 points in the regressed area.

**Cost, fit and risks.** About $1.5 for one candidate, built only when its trigger fires. RL deltas are about 10× smaller than SFT deltas ([2607.22039](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22039v1)), so the merge is well-posed. The risk is low. The cost is a wasted candidate that adds selection noise.

### 5. Small-α extrapolation is a conditional lottery ticket

**Mechanism.** ExPO: θ = θ_rl1 + α(θ_rl1 − θ_v4), with α=0.2 (λ=1.2), only if per-domain training reward is still clearly rising at the final step.

**Evidence.** At 7-8B, ExPO gave +0.8 to +4.4 AlpacaEval LC points after DPO or RLHF, and helped undertrained models most. On reasoning it was mixed: GSM8K **fell 7.7 points** for llama3-8b-iter ([ExPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16792v2)). For RLVR specifically, extrapolating past a finished run on Qwen3-8B gave **88.0 versus 88.5** at the real endpoint, then 85.6 further out, and Qwen3-4B collapsed to 50.9-73.6 ([RELEX](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21468v1)).

**Expected gain for us.** **−1 to +0.5.** It is cheap (about $1.5), so include it only under its trigger, and drop it if any area loses more than 1 point or length rises. If the trigger does not fire, do not build it. Each extra candidate adds roughly 0.3-0.5 points of selection optimism.

## Reward source: keep RLVR on every graded rollout

All grading is programmatic, and the program is the highest-fidelity reward available. The head-to-heads all point one way. Gating the verifiable IF reward on an RM score took Tulu-3-8B from **IFEval 92.2 to 86.1 and IFBench 44.6 to 30**, while raising AlpacaEval 21.3 to 31.6, a metric we are not graded on ([Pyatkin et al., App. E](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02833v3)). DPO on RM or AI-judge preferences *lowered* IFEval for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, from 77.3 to 75.2-76.0 ([RLCF](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18624v2)). RM-only online training dropped an 8B model's MATH-500 from 47.4 to 35.0 ([Lanchantin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21495v1)). Judges are also exploitable: bare openers like "Solution:" reach false-positive rates up to about 90% on 70B judges, and an RLVR policy trained against one collapsed to very short responses ([One Token to Fool](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08794v1)). The verifier signal, by contrast, is large. IF-RLVR with GRPO took Tulu-3-8B-DPO from 81.1 to 92.2 IFEval, and from the same data GRPO beat DPO by about 9-10 IFEval points ([Pyatkin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02833v3)).

So "RLAIF vs RLHF" is mostly moot for us: both are preference proxies, and neither is the metric. Three narrow uses of AI feedback remain defensible, and the rl1 spec already covers or rejects each. Hybrid code+LLM verification (VerIF) helps only where constraints are *soft* ([VerIF](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09942v1)), and IFEval's constraints are all programmatic. A TinyV-style fallback verifier fixes math false negatives ([TinyV](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14625v2)), but rl1's math reward *is* the graded scorer, so "fixing" its false negatives would teach answer formats the grader rejects. A **veto-only** judge that can only zero the reward of a verifier-passed but degenerate IF answer is untested. It would cost about $2-4 with a nano-class judge on verifier-passed IF rollouts. Build it only if the trap-prompt hack rate trips the spec's pause rule. OLMo 3's pattern, programmatic IF reward on IF prompts plus an LM-judge reward only on *separate* chat prompts, kept chat quality "without compromising IFEval" ([OLMo 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13961)). It matters only if the hidden sets include free-form quality, and the brief says they do not. The 397B is better spent as a token-level teacher (method 2) than as a judge.

## Methods to skip, with reasons

| Method | One-line reason to skip |
|---|---|
| WARP multi-run SLERP + LITI | Needs M ≥ 2 full RL runs per iteration; evidence is from ~9k-step Gemma-7B RLHF runs, with no fixed-compute comparison ([WARP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16768)) |
| Several short seeds merged vs one run | No equal-compute RLVR evidence; 100-step seeds are still early in the reward curve |
| ProRL reference/optimizer resets | Fixes KL domination after hundreds to thousands of steps; rl1 has no KL loss term and is only 200 steps ([ProRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24864v1)) |
| EMA-policy KL anchor (WARP, EMA-PG) | rl1 has no KL term to re-anchor; EMA-PG evidence is 1.5B ([EMA-PG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04417)) |
| Elastic Reset | Changes the training loop; no 7-9B RLVR numbers recovered ([Elastic Reset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07551)); its post-hoc analogue is method 4 |
| Online Merging Optimizers | Tested only with DPO/IPO/KTO, and not LoRA-compatible ([Lu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17931v1)) |
| LUFFY in-group off-policy traces | Needs a custom shaping loss and teacher behaviour logprobs we lack for GPT answers ([LUFFY](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14945)) |
| Always-on plain NLL auxiliary loss | Worst LUFFY variant: −5 ID, −11 OOD vs pure RL, and costlier ([LUFFY](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14945)) |
| RAFT / expert iteration instead of RL | Matches GRPO (56.1 vs 56.3) but plateaus near iteration 100 from entropy collapse ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11343)); self-SFT hurts IFEval |
| Training IF/math/code specialist LoRAs to merge | Three extra SFT runs; data mixing beat all merges once formats varied ([LoRA Soups](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13025v2)); only Task Arithmetic reliably helps in the wild ([Hitit et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2511.21437)) |
| Soups of v1..v4 | ExPO authors report separately trained SFT pairs do not benefit ([ExPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16792v2)); v4's "earlier version solved it" items are mostly flipping noise |
| TIES / DARE / DELLA / Model Stock | Built for multi-expert merges of full-FT models; no 7-9B LoRA post-training evidence on our benchmarks |
| RELEX / NExt trajectory extrapolation | Needs fitted trajectories from 500-step runs; NExt notes LoRA-RLVR parameters are not all linear ([NExt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11446)) |
| RLAIF / judge reward / multiplicative judge gate | Measured −6 IFEval and −15 IFBench ([Pyatkin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02833v3)) |
| AI-preference DPO before RL | 0 to +1.3 IFEval at best at 7B, sometimes negative ([RLCF](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18624v2)) |

## The selection protocol, fixed before the run

The protocol below fits the rl1 spec's dev cadence (greedy dev every 20 steps, saves every 10). It adds smoothing, a pre-registered merge set and a merge-adoption rule. Every input is a private dev split or the vault. Public IFEval, MATH-500, MBPP and HumanEval are never used to choose. The spec's Section 10 lists public scores and HumanEval under "confirmation on independent data", and they must stay that way: report them only after the final choice is frozen, never as a tie-breaker. A confirmation that can change the decision is selection.

| Step | What | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Pre-register | Before step 1 of the pilot, commit (git) the candidate list, metric and adoption rule below | No candidate added after looking at dev |
| 1. Trajectory screen | Dev sum (math + code + IF) for each 20-step checkpoint (k ≈ 10-11), greedy | Compute a centred 3-point moving average; apply the spec's "suspect" filters (length > 2×, truncation, KL, gate/guard rates, trap hacks) and drop suspect checkpoints |
| 2. Native pick | Argmax of the smoothed dev sum among non-suspect checkpoints | Subject to every area within 1 SE of v4 and the pooled unseen IF not below v4 − 1 SE (spec rule) |
| 3. Build merges (max 3) | M1: ΔW uniform average of the 3-5 saved checkpoints (10-step spacing) centred on the native pick's window, never including steps < 50. M2: v4↔rl1 λ=0.6, only if an area is > 1 SE below v4 or truncation rose. M3: ExPO λ=1.2, only if reward is still rising over the last 30 steps | SVD truncation error per layer reported; each merge passes the upload round trip |
| 4. Paired comparison | Each merge vs the native pick and vs v4: McNemar per area, paired bootstrap (10k, resampling within task) on the dev sum, Holm across candidates | Adopt M1 if its dev sum is not lower than the native pick and no area falls > 1 SE, since averaging's prior is positive and it lowers variance. Adopt M2 or M3 only if its margin exceeds the expected-max optimism (~1.3-1.6 σ for k = 6-11) and format and truncation rates do not worsen |
| 5. Vault, once | At most 2 finalists: the step-4 choice and the safest fallback (the native pick) | Vault picks between those two only. If the reversal is smaller than the paired SE, keep the pre-declared default (step 4's choice) |
| 6. Freeze, then report | Clear TTL on the final checkpoint; then run public IFEval / MATH-500 / MBPP / HumanEval once | Public scores are reported with paired CIs against v4 and never re-open selection |

Two details keep this honest. First, the winner's dev score is biased, so report the **vault** score as the estimate of the chosen model, and state that dev-sum differences below about 3.5 points per area are not evidence. Second, grading includes hidden held-out sets, so a dev-sum gain bought by an IFEval loss is a bad trade. Tulu 3's unseen-suite analysis found that models "generally overfit to IFEval" ([Tulu 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15124v4)), which is why the per-area floor applies before the sum is maximized.

| How large is the factor-averaging vs ΔW-averaging gap, and the rank-32 SVD truncation error, on our actual checkpoints? | Decides whether the SVD path is lossless enough | Few seconds of CPU per layer on downloaded adapters |

## Conclusion

The literature's large numbers for merging and hybrid training come from settings where the model had a lot left to learn and plenty of steps to learn it. Our case is the opposite: a strong SFT model, a 200-step LoRA run and dev sets that cannot see effects below about 3.5 points. Under those conditions the winning strategy is to spend money on signal, not on candidates. Gated SFT and, if approved, a masked teacher term put gradient on the prompts where plain RLVR has none, which is where the remaining points are. Checkpoint methods are best treated as variance control: one exact tail average adopted by default unless it visibly breaks something, and one interpolation held in reserve for a regression.

For the course report, the distinctive story is not "we merged models." It is that the team quantified the winner's curse on its own dev sets, pre-registered a small candidate set because of it, implemented an exact ΔW-space LoRA merge with SVD back to rank 32 on a hosted service, and turned zero-variance RL groups into supervised signal. Each piece is simple, defensible under the no-test-set rule, and uncommon in course projects. If the vault shows all of it within noise of the best native checkpoint, that is also a clean, reportable finding: checkpoint methods add little on top of short RLVR from a strong SFT start.
